# Privacy policy and terms: additions for the new direction

**Draft for Tikka's lawyer. Not live.** Prepared 26 November 2026. The current privacy policy and terms (usetikka.com/privacy and /terms) were cleared on 10 November 2026 and only cover drops. Since then Tikka has added, or is about to add:

- **Bookings with deposits** (live since 20 to 23 November): customers book a free time on a business's page, sometimes paying a deposit.
- **Pay links** (live since 22 November): a business sends a customer a link to pay an amount.
- **Coming with the new version** (built, not yet released): home visits, a waitlist for full days, a record of each customer's visits, spending, notes and birthday kept for the business, sales the business records at the counter, receipts, and alerts on the owner's phone.

Below is proposed wording in the same plain style as the current text. Each item says whether it's **new** or **changes** an existing section. Questions for you are at the end.

---

## Privacy policy

### Intro (changes)

> This explains what Tikka collects when you claim a Tikka, book or pay a business, or run your business on Tikka, who can see it, and how to have it deleted. The short version: the business you claim from, book with or pay gets your first name and WhatsApp number so it can serve you and follow up; nobody else does; and you can ask us to delete your details at any time.

### When you book a business (new)

> When you book a time on a business's page, we collect:
> - the first name and WhatsApp number you type in;
> - the service, any options and extras you pick, the day and time, and your answers to the business's questions about the booking;
> - for a home visit, the address you give;
> - if the booking takes a deposit, the email you give for the payment receipt, and how much you paid.
>
> We use these details to hold your time, show you your booking pass, let the business prepare and reach you, and let you move or cancel within the business's rules. Booking makes you one of that business's customers: it sees your first name, number, booking, any answers and, for a home visit, your address.
>
> Your booking pass is a private link. Anyone you share it with can see your booking, but never your number or address.

### When you pay a business (new)

> Deposits and pay links are paid through Paystack. Paystack collects your card or bank details; Tikka never sees them. We keep what you paid, when, what it was for, and the email you gave for the receipt.
>
> After you pay, you get a receipt link. It shows the business, what you paid for and each payment, never your number. Anyone you share the link with can see it.

### The waitlist (new)

> If a day is full and you ask to join the waitlist, we keep your first name, WhatsApp number, and the day and service you wanted, so the business can tell you if a place opens up. The business sees them. We delete them 30 days after the day you wanted. Joining a waitlist doesn't book you a time or sign you up for offers.

### What a business keeps about its customers (new)

> Each business on Tikka has a customer list: everyone who booked, bought from it or claimed its Tikka, one entry per WhatsApp number. For each customer it shows their visits and bookings, what they spent and still owe and how they paid, and anything the business adds: notes (for example "sensitive scalp") and a birthday (day and month, never the year).
>
> The business decides what to keep and uses it to serve you. We store it for the business and don't use it for anything else. Only the business owner sees it, never its staff. You can ask the business, or us, to see or delete it (see "Your rights").

### Who sees your details (changes: replaces the first two points)

> - The business you claimed from, booked with or paid sees your first name, WhatsApp number and profile name, your Tikkas, bookings, answers and address for a home visit, what you paid and owe, and any notes it keeps about you. It can message you on WhatsApp from its own phone, and it may keep you on its customer list.
> - The business's staff see your first name, your Tikka or booking, and the service at the counter. They never see your phone number, your address or the business's notes.

### If you run a business on Tikka (changes: adds two paragraphs)

> If you turn on alerts on your phone, we keep the address your browser gives us to send them (new customers, bookings, payments, and the evening and weekly money summary). Staff never get these alerts. You can turn them off on that phone at any time.
>
> The sales, balances, expenses and regular costs you record are your business's own records. We keep them for you and don't share them.

### Counting visits without knowing who you are (changes: adds one sentence)

> When someone scans a business card made on Tikka, we count one scan per browser per day the same way, without knowing who it is.

### Services we use (changes)

> - **Paystack:** takes payments from businesses, and payments from customers to a business (deposits and pay links). The money goes straight to the business's bank account.
> - **Web push services (Google, Apple, Mozilla):** deliver alerts to business owners' phones.

### How long we keep things (changes)

> We keep claim, booking, payment and customer-list details for as long as the business uses Tikka and 12 months after that, so the business can see its history and we can deal with questions. Waitlist details go 30 days after the day asked for. Visit counts never had names in them.
>
> When you ask us to delete your details, we delete your claims, bookings, waitlist entries, notes and messages. Payment amounts stay in the business's records, without your name or number, so its accounts stay right.

### Your rights (changes: the "to ask" line)

> To ask, email hello@usetikka.com or message Tikka on WhatsApp, and tell us the WhatsApp number you claimed or booked with.

## Terms

### About these terms (changes)

> By claiming a Tikka, booking or paying a business through Tikka, or creating a business account, you agree to them. Part A is for customers; part B is for businesses.

### A. Booking a business (new)

> - Each business sets its own booking rules (for example how late you can be, and how much notice to move). You see them before you book, and they apply to your booking.
> - Some bookings take a deposit. It's paid to the business through Paystack; Tikka never holds the money.
> - You can move your booking from your booking pass if the business's rules allow (usually with 24 hours' notice). Moving keeps your deposit with the booking. Cancelling doesn't refund it.
> - Where a price says "from", the business confirms the final price with you. The deposit is on the starting price.
> - For a home visit, be at the address you gave at the time you booked.

### A. Paying a business (new)

> - Deposits and pay links go straight to the business. Tikka doesn't refund payments. If you and the business agree a refund, the business sends it to you.
> - Your receipt is proof of payment from the business. It isn't a tax invoice.
> - If a business takes your money and doesn't provide what you paid for, tell us at hello@usetikka.com and we'll take it up with them.

### B. Taking bookings and payments (new)

> - Honour the bookings you accept, on the rules you showed when the customer booked. Keep your booking rules fair and clear.
> - Payments from customers go to your own bank account through Paystack. Tikka takes 1% of each payment, and Paystack takes its fee. Prices are shown before anything is charged.
> - Tikka doesn't refund customers. If you agree a refund, send it from your bank. You're responsible for disputes with your customers about your service.
> - Before switching on payments, we check your ID and bank account. We may set a maximum per payment, and pause payments if we see fraud or a broken rule.
> - Receipts made on Tikka are proof of payment, not tax invoices. You're responsible for your own tax.

### B. Your customers' details (changes: adds three points)

> - The customer list, notes and waitlist are for serving your customers. Only use waitlist numbers to tell people a place is free.
> - Keep notes respectful and only about the service, and don't write anything you wouldn't be comfortable with the customer seeing: they can ask for a copy.
> - When a customer asks to be deleted, we delete what Tikka holds about them, including your notes.

## Questions for you

1. **Who's responsible for the customer list?** We've written it as the business deciding (controller) and Tikka storing it for them (processor). Do the terms need a data processing clause for businesses?
2. **Deposits that aren't refunded.** Customers see the business's rules and "the deposit is not refundable" before paying. Is that enough under the FCCPA 2018, or does the wording need more?
3. **How long to keep payment records.** We say 12 months after the business leaves Tikka. Do payment or tax rules (Paystack's, CBN, FIRS) require longer?
4. **Forgetting someone.** We keep payment amounts in the business's records without the person's name or number. Is that acceptable under the NDPA 2023?
5. **Health details in notes.** Beauty businesses note things like allergies or a sensitive scalp. Does that need its own wording, or a limit on what owners can write?
6. **Links anyone can open.** Booking passes and receipts open to anyone with the link (never showing the number or address). Is "keep the link private" enough?
7. **Age.** The policy says Tikka is for people aged 18 and over. Should booking and paying say the same?
