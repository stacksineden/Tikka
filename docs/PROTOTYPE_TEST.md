# Prototype test script: owner setup and Today

For anyone on the team running a test of the owner prototype (design/prototypes/owner-v2.html, link: https://claude.ai/artifact/UQY1oqiJfqSQ9G57dUS2MT; share it from the page's Share menu first). About 30 minutes per owner. The goal is to find where owners get stuck, not to sell to them.

## Who to test with

3 to 5 owners: the makeup artist, Divora, and two or three from the pipeline. Mix it up: one who uses Instagram a lot, one who doesn't, and if possible one with a helper or a busy shop.

## Before you start

- Use your phone or theirs. Open the link and check it loads on the start screen.
- Have a notebook (or voice notes, if they agree) to write down what they say and where they stop.
- Pick a quiet time for them, not in the middle of a customer.

## What to say at the start (2 minutes)

> "Thank you for your time. We're making Tikka easier to use and we want to see it through your eyes. This is an early version: some buttons don't do anything yet, and nothing you type is saved. We're testing the app, not you, so there are no wrong answers. If something is confusing, that's our fault and exactly what we need to know. Please say what you're thinking out loud as you go."

Don't use the words "drop", "Tikka card", "claim", "promo" or "gift" when explaining. Say "your website", "bookings", "deposit", "discount".

## Part 1: Their week today (5 minutes, before they touch the phone)

Ask, and let them talk:
1. "How do customers book you now?" (DMs, calls, walk-ins?)
2. "What happens when someone doesn't show up?"
3. "How do you keep track of who paid and who still owes you?"
4. "Do you know how much you made last week? How do you work it out?"
5. "What do you spend money on every week for the business?"

## Part 2: Tasks (15 minutes)

Hand them the phone. Read each task, then stay quiet. Don't point, don't explain, don't rescue them unless they're stuck for more than a minute. If they ask "what do I do?", answer: "What would you try?"

**Task 1: Set up.** On the start screen, tap "1. Set up a new business".
> "Imagine you just heard about this and want a website for your business. Set it up the way you would for real." (Tell them to make up the ID and bank details, never real ones.)

Watch for:
- Do they understand "What do you do?" and pick their trade straight away?
- On "Your services": do they untick what they don't do and change prices? Do they find the choices (short / medium / long)? Do the starting prices look right for their city?
- Do they understand "How many customers can you do at the same time?"
- On "Your page is live!": do they look at the preview? Do they want to share it?
- On deposits: do they hesitate at the ID or bank step? Do they believe "the money goes straight to your bank"?
- Do the booking rules sound like them?

**Task 2: The morning.** Go back to the start screen, tap "2. A day at the shop".
> "It's morning and you've just opened the app. Tell me what you see."

Then ask: "Who is coming today? How much will you collect?"

**Task 3: A customer arrives and pays.**
> "Ada Nwosu just arrived. Later she pays you in cash. Show me what you'd do."

Watch: do they find Arrived, then Paid, then Cash?

**Task 4: A walk-in.**
> "Someone walked in without booking, got cornrows and paid ₦5,000 by transfer. Record it."

Watch: do they find "New sale"? Does the part-payment idea make sense?

**Task 5: A reminder.**
> "You want to remind Tolu about her 5pm appointment."

**Task 6: Money.**
> "How much did you keep this week? Add ₦4,000 you spent on transport today."

## Part 3: Questions after (5 minutes)

1. "What was the most confusing part?"
2. "What did you like most?"
3. "Is anything missing that you'd need to use this every day?"
4. "Would you use the Today screen and record your sales here? Why or why not?"
5. "Would you take deposits? What would stop you?"
6. "If this was free, and Tikka took 1% only when customers paid you online, would you use it? Would you tell another business about it?"

Thank them. If they're not on Tikka yet and they're keen, offer to set up their real page together.

## When all the sessions are done

Put the notes together. Anything that tripped up 2 or more owners gets fixed in the prototype before we build. Bring the notes to the next planning session.
